Automatic tuning experiment

The computer tries thousands of rule settings on the practice recordings, then checks the winners on brand-new recordings. The same seed always gives the same recordings and the same results.

Why test on so many recordings?

If you tune the rules on just one recording of each situation, they can fit those exact recordings perfectly and still fail on a slightly faster walker, a phone in a bag, or a noisier sensor. It's like memorizing the answers to one practice test. So the tuner practises on 60 varied recordings, and then it's checked on 60 new recordings it has never seen. The card "Tuned on only 6 recordings" shows what happens if you skip this: a great practice score, then a drop on new recordings.

Walking, running, shaking and the bus are simulated with realistic 3-axis sensor data. "Use my phone's real sensor" works when this page is opened on a phone, and the data never leaves your device.
